Map extent 84.52–85.60°E, 27.70–28.45°N, following the Trishuli from Mugling and Galchhi upstream through Rasuwagadhi into the Lhende valley (Tibet), where a section of glacier at ~5,200 m collapsed on 26 Aug (per ICIMOD/USGS reporting) and triggered the flood — the origin marker is approximate, placed on the glacier basin ~4 km north of Langtang Lirung (~13 km ENE of Rasuwagadhi) where the 27 Aug scene shows fresh disturbance through a cloud gap; the high valley is partly cloud-covered in that scene. PlanetScope 3.8 m imagery from Planet's open disaster data (CC-BY-NC-4.0): the pre-event scene 20260527_053226_41_254a (contrast-stretched) and full-res flood-morning scene 20260826_050135_34_255f cover the band 85.10–85.20°E, 27.83–27.95°N; the flood-morning view is extended across most of the extent by a 9-scene mosaic from two passes (~10:46 and ~11:30 NPT, rendered ~20 m) — heavy monsoon cloud, with the surge visible in gaps; the day-2 option is a 5-scene PlanetScope mosaic from 28 Aug (~10:42 NPT) with the clearest view of the upper valley and debris corridor; in both, areas outside the swaths fall back to the 27 Aug backdrop. Sentinel-2 10 m imagery (resampled to ~20 m here) covers the full extent: pre-event S2C 45RUL/45RUM (3 Jun) and day-after S2B 45RUL/45RUM (27 Aug, ~10:56 NPT) © ESA Copernicus, via the AWS open Sentinel-2 COG archive. The flood-morning scene carries thin cloud over the town; the 27 Aug scene is mostly clear but coarser. The contour overlay is derived from the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM (© ESA/Airbus, 30 m): vector lines that reveal progressively with zoom (1000 → 500 → 200 → 100 → 50 → 10 m), with elevation labels placed only on terrain gentler than 25° — index lines first, 100 m and 50 m labels appearing as you zoom in.
