Map extent 84.52–85.60°E, 27.70–28.45°N, following the Trishuli from Mugling and Galchhi upstream through Rasuwagadhi into the Lhende valley (Tibet), where a section of glacier at ~5,200 m collapsed on 26 Aug (per ICIMOD/USGS reporting) and triggered the flood — the origin marker is approximate, placed on the glacier basin ~4 km north of Langtang Lirung (~13 km ENE of Rasuwagadhi) where the 27 Aug scene shows fresh disturbance through a cloud gap; the high valley is partly cloud-covered in that scene. PlanetScope 3.8 m imagery from Planet's open disaster data (CC-BY-NC-4.0): the pre-event scene 20260527_053226_41_254a (contrast-stretched) and full-res flood-morning scene 20260826_050135_34_255f cover the band 85.10–85.20°E, 27.83–27.95°N; the flood-morning view is extended across most of the extent by a 9-scene mosaic from two passes (~10:46 and ~11:30 NPT, rendered ~20 m) — heavy monsoon cloud, with the surge visible in gaps; the day-2 option is a 5-scene PlanetScope mosaic from 28 Aug (~10:42 NPT) with the clearest view of the upper valley and debris corridor; in both, areas outside the swaths fall back to the 27 Aug backdrop. The 31 Aug option is a pair of SkySat collects (~15:10 NPT, 0.65 m GSD) from Betrawati down through Trisuli Bazar and Bidur — the only sub-metre view of the downstream reach; the southern frame is largely clear over the river and town while the northern frame over Battar and Betrawati is mostly cloud. The collect's own georeferencing sat ~500 m east and ~800 m south of the map grid, so each frame has been re-registered here with a thin-plate-spline warp through ~80–200 tie points matched to the pre-event PlanetScope scene (residual a few metres over clear ground; the collect was taken ~9° off-nadir, so a few metres of terrain-related shift can remain on steep slopes). The 1 Sep option is three Pelican scenes (~10:51 NPT, 0.56 m GSD) from Syabrubesi to Rasuwagadhi, re-imaging the 27 Aug Pelican footprint, 47–84 % cloud. Both are rendered at ~0.73 m and streamed as map tiles fetched only for the area in view; outside their footprints the 27 Aug Sentinel-2 shows through. Sentinel-2 10 m imagery (resampled to ~20 m here) covers the full extent: pre-event S2C 45RUL/45RUM (3 Jun) and day-after S2B 45RUL/45RUM (27 Aug, ~10:56 NPT) © ESA Copernicus, via the AWS open Sentinel-2 COG archive. The flood-morning scene carries thin cloud over the town; the 27 Aug scene is mostly clear but coarser. The contour overlay is derived from the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM (© ESA/Airbus, 30 m): vector lines that reveal progressively with zoom (1000 → 500 → 200 → 100 → 50 → 10 m), with elevation labels placed only on terrain gentler than 25° — index lines first, 100 m and 50 m labels appearing as you zoom in. The 10 m lines are streamed as map tiles, fetched only for the area in view. The roads overlay shows trunk/primary/secondary highways (amber), tertiary branch roads at closer zoom, and bridge symbols (][ = road bridges, )( = suspension footbridges) from © OpenStreetMap contributors — alignments are pre-flood. Bridges drawn red cross the Lhende–Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood stem upstream of Galchhi and are presumed destroyed or damaged (exposure-based, not field-verified); amber bridges are outside the flood path, and road sections drawn red (within ~175 m of the flood stem) are presumed flood-damaged on the same exposure basis. The rivers overlay (also OSM) washes rivers in cyan, with the Lhende–Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood stem highlighted red. The separate flood overlay outlines the flood extent — the widened debris channel traced from the post-event imagery (27 Aug Sentinel-2, with the 28 Aug PlanetScope mosaic filling cloud), classified within ~500 m of the river and generalised to ~30 m; it is approximate, breaks where all post-event scenes are clouded, and is thin and patchy in the hazy uppermost Lhende valley. Coordinates shown are approximate.
